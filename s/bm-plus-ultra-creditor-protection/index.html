Plus Ultra seeks creditor protection, cuts most routes and 679 jobs

Reported · 3 min

Open in the full site →

Plus Ultra Líneas Aéreas filed a pre-insolvency notice with Madrid Commercial Court No. 8 on 13 November 2026, according to Aviacionline, giving it three months, extendable by three, to agree a restructuring plan with creditors. The airline served a collective redundancy notice on 12 November covering 679 employees, the outlet reports, citing the UGT union, and also plans a temporary layoff procedure and a smaller fleet of Airbus A330s.

AeroTime reports that scheduled flying would shrink to Caracas–Madrid and Tenerife North, with most activity shifting to ACMI wet-leasing, and that the airline blames fuel prices linked to the Middle East conflict. Aviacionline gives operating losses of €31.08 million between February and August and projected 2026 fuel spending of €104.83 million against a €69.13 million budget. AeroTime describes the loss figure as unverified Spanish media reporting.

Aviacionline adds that the airline asked Spain's state holding company SEPI to defer by two years repayment of the first €19 million of a €53 million 2021 rescue package, and that the chairman and chief executive lost their powers in July and later stepped down. AeroTime says Volotea also filed for creditor protection in September 2026. Both outlets rely partly on unnamed or unverified sources, so the figures are best read as reported.

What it means: Airline restructuring and turnaround work, fleet and ACMI wet-lease strategy, and labour-negotiation support; read-across for carriers exposed to fuel costs.

What we don't know yet

  • Final restructuring plan and number of redundancies not known
  • Whether the Lima and Buenos Aires route halts become permanent is unclear
