Steer buys US ports consultancy Mercator, its fourth deal since 2021

Reported · 3 min

Open in the full site →

Steer, the UK-headquartered transport and infrastructure consultancy, has acquired Mercator, a US maritime and ports adviser, Consultancy.uk reported on 18 November 2026. The deal strengthens Steer's work in ports, maritime transport and freight advisory.

Mercator has been based in Kirkland, Washington, since 2000. It advises investors, port authorities, terminal operators, shipping companies and logistics businesses on strategy, commercial matters and transactions. The article says it has completed more than 150 commercial due diligence projects and advised over 40 investors and financial institutions. Named projects include advice to the Ports of Seattle and Tacoma on forming the Northwest Seaport Alliance, to the Quebec Port Authority on a new container terminal, and to a Macquarie-led consortium on its acquisition of Long Beach Container Terminal.

Steer was founded in 1978 and has 29 offices across Europe, North America, Latin America and Asia. This is its fourth acquisition since 2021, following Fourth Economy in the US and Amberside Advisors and Evolution Infrastructure in the UK. Mercator will join Steer's North American division, keep its brand and keep its leadership. Steer chief executive Hugh Jones said the deal will strengthen the firm's offer beyond North America.

Price and deal value were not disclosed. The deal matters to clients because port ownership and terminal investment are active deal markets, and due-diligence capacity in that niche is thin.

What it means: Port, terminal and shipping strategy, commercial due diligence and transaction advisory; a larger maritime bench for infrastructure investors.
