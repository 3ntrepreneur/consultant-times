Anthropic prices IPO at $83 a share, valuing it near $1.5 trillion

Reported · 4 min

Open in the full site →

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 a share on Wednesday 18 November 2026, above the $70 to $78 range it set when it launched its roadshow on 10 November, according to an Associated Press report carried by PBS NewsHour. The shares are due to begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ANT.

AP reports that 980 million Class A shares are being sold: 956,351,354 by the company and 23,648,646 by selling stockholders. The original plan, in Anthropic's own launch announcement, was 825 million shares. AP puts total proceeds at about $81 billion and the implied valuation at $1.5 trillion, and says that makes it the largest IPO on record, ahead of SpaceX, which raised about $75 billion in June. AP does not say whether the $81 billion is gross or net of fees.

The article also cites two figures from the offering documents that matter to advisers: $6.2 billion of debt, and about $900 billion of committed compute and infrastructure spending, of which about $70 billion falls in the next 12 months. Both are as reported by AP; we have not read the prospectus itself.

Anthropic's roadshow announcement, which we read earlier this month, also described a concurrent private placement of $26 billion to strategic partners at the IPO price. A market wrap on Yahoo Finance said seven technology companies had agreed to buy that block. We could not confirm the partner list or final size of the placement in a primary document, so it is not treated as settled here.

For consultants the story is less the headline number than the commitments behind it. A buyer with $900 billion of committed spending, and a valuation that depends on it being filled, shapes the demand for data-centre, power and chip supply chains, and for the advisory work that sits around them. For listed-company clients, the pricing above range is a data point on appetite for AI exposure, but a first-day move says little about durability.
