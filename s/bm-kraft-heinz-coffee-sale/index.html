Kraft Heinz sells North American coffee business to One Rock Capital for $650 million

Reported · 2 min

Open in the full site →

According to Bloomberg Law, the deal values the unit at $650 million, with Kraft Heinz retaining a 30% non-controlling stake and closing expected in mid-2027. ConsumerGoods.com lists the brands as Maxwell House, Gevalia, Nabob, Yuban, Tassimo, Ethical Bean and Sanka, with about 250 full-time employees and plants in Jacksonville and Vancouver expected to transfer.

ConsumerGoods.com reports Kraft Heinz expects net proceeds of about $500 million to pay down debt and that CEO Steve Cahillane called it a step in unlocking portfolio value. Bloomberg Law says the sale narrows the portfolio as the company seeks to restart growth.

ConsumerGoods.com adds the move sits within a wider restructuring that includes consolidating supply-chain functions and a shift away from a planned company split.

What it means: Carve-out, TSA and stranded-cost work for the coffee unit, plus portfolio-focus and supply-chain consolidation support for Kraft Heinz.
