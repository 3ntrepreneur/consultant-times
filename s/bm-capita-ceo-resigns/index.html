Capita CEO Adolfo Hernandez resigns amid pension-scheme backlog

Reported · 3 min

Open in the full site →

Capita chief executive Adolfo Hernandez has stepped down with immediate effect, and chief financial officer Pablo Andres replaces him, according to Consultancy.uk on 24 November 2026. Hernandez took the job at the start of 2024, succeeding Jon Lewis.

The report links the move to Capita's handling of the Civil Service Pension Scheme, about 1.7 million members, which it took over in December 2025 under a £239 million contract signed by the previous government. The backlog was above 86,000 cases at handover and above 120,000 by early February 2026, and about 8,500 members reportedly had payments delayed. A February government recovery plan added an emergency interest-free loan scheme, 150 civil servants and 100 more Capita staff.

The report says Capita has also warned that its 2027 profit range would be £85 million to £95 million, at least £25 million below earlier expectations. Innovation minister Lilian Greenwood called the problems a 'debacle' days before the resignation, the public accounts committee opened an inquiry earlier this month, and a National Audit Office report is due in the coming months. Ministers have noted the option of returning the contract to government management.

What it means: Government outsourcing recovery, contract-performance reviews, operational turnaround and CEO-transition support; public bodies reassessing large outsourced service contracts.

What we don't know yet

  • Whether the contract will be retained or returned to government
  • Findings of the National Audit Office and committee inquiry
  • Capita's own statement on the profit range timing
