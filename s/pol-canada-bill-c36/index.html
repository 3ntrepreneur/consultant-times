Canada tables Bill C-36, its third try at a new private-sector privacy law

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

On 15 June 2026 Minister of AI and Digital Innovation Evan Solomon tabled Bill C-36, the Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act. Fasken calls it the third attempt in six years to reform federal private-sector privacy law, after Bills C-11 and C-27.

Two features stand out against C-27. Fasken says there is no stand-alone AI statute this time, and oversight moves to a new Digital Safety and Data Protection Commission of Canada instead of the Privacy Commissioner plus a tribunal. Gowling adds that the Commission would take over private-sector privacy authority from the Privacy Commissioner's office.

Al Jazeera's coverage quotes an Oxford law professor arguing the bill still misses how AI changes where privacy harms occur.

What it means: Any client handling Canadian personal data, including AI vendors and banks, should plan for stricter rules and a new oversight body.

What we don't know yet

  • Whether the bill passes before the next election.
  • What AI-specific rules will arrive in separate bills.
