Home Depot promotes CFO Richard McPhail to CEO

Confirmed · 3 min

Open in the full site →

Home Depot said on 17 November 2026 that chief financial officer Richard McPhail will become chief executive officer, effective 23 November, succeeding Ted Decker. According to the company's release, filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, McPhail, who joined Home Depot in 2005 and has been CFO since September 2019, will also be elected to the board. Decker will step down as president and CEO and continue as chair of the board.

The release names two further changes on the same date. Ann-Marie Campbell, described as a 41-year veteran of the company, becomes president and chief operating officer. Jordan Broggi, a 13-year veteran who was most recently executive vice president of interconnected retail, becomes executive vice president and CFO. In effect the top three roles all pass to long-serving insiders.

Asked about the reason, the company pointed to succession planning. The Atlanta Journal-Constitution quotes a spokesperson calling the change part of normal and ongoing succession planning, and WSB-TV quotes independent lead director Greg Brenneman referring to the board's ongoing, active succession planning. In the release, Decker says he looks forward to supporting the new team during the transition.

The announcement came on the morning Home Depot reported third-quarter results. WSB-TV reports that sales rose about $2 billion, almost 5%, from the same period in 2025, and quotes McPhail saying the quarter exceeded the company's expectations. Neither the release nor the two press reports we read gives earnings per share, comparable sales or guidance, so we do not report them here.

For advisers, a CFO-to-CEO promotion at a $100-billion-plus retailer tends to signal continuity in strategy and capital allocation rather than a reset. It also opens a sequence of work: the new CFO's finance organisation, the operating committee under a new COO, and any change in how the company talks to investors about housing-market sensitivity, professional customers and its acquisition-led supply-chain strategy. These are our readings of the structure, not company statements.

What we do not yet know: whether strategy, capital-return policy or the company's financial targets will change under McPhail; how investors reacted, since none of the sources we read reports a share-price move; and what Decker's role as chair will involve in practice.
