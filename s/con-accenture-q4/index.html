Accenture ends fiscal 2026 on a record for big contracts, with AI work broadening

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

Accenture reported fourth-quarter revenue of $18.7 billion, up 6%, and earnings of $3.29 a share. Bookings were $22.2 billion, including $12.8 billion of managed services. CEO Julie Sweet cited a quarterly record of 141 client bookings worth at least $100 million. Fiscal 2026 revenue was $74.2 billion.

Sweet said AI has not led to headcount cuts and that the company is upskilling and hiring in data and AI. It ended the year with nearly 110,000 AI and data professionals, and more than 400 clients started their first advanced-AI projects with Accenture during the year, according to National CIO Review. InMoney notes consulting bookings eased from about $10.3 billion in Q3 to $9.4 billion, and Accenture no longer reports separate AI bookings.

What it means: Demand for AI-led transformation is real, but it is shifting towards data foundations and managed services. Consulting bookings eased from the prior quarter.

What we don't know yet

  • Accenture no longer discloses AI revenue, so AI's share is unclear.
