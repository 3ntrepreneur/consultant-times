Nvidia board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO 11 days after he resigned

Confirmed · 5 min

Open in the full site →

Nvidia's board on Monday 23 November 2026 unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately, according to the company's filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. Huang resigned on 12 November, as covered earlier in this edition. Chief financial officer Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, returns to her CFO role. Harvey C. Jones remains board chair.

In the filing, Nvidia says the board received input from senior levels of the US government and concluded that Huang's return poses no national security risk. Engadget reports that the board met late on Sunday and voted on Monday, that Jones had said days earlier the company did not intend to reinstate Huang, and that President Donald Trump, speaking to CNBC, said he had influenced the board. Nvidia's filing does not mention the president.

The board paired the decision with governance changes. A committee of independent directors, advised by independent counsel, is investigating reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, and Huang supports the work and will cooperate, the filing says. The board is also creating a National Security and Export Control Committee made up only of independent directors, and is hiring a chief compliance officer who will report to the nominating and corporate governance committee.

The market reaction was large. Yahoo Finance's headline put the one-day rise in Nvidia shares above 30%, and the Fox News live blog reported a gain of about 31% that restored most of the value lost after the resignation. Those figures come from press reports of Monday's trading, not from Nvidia.

The decision sits inside a wider export-control story. Tech Times reports that the US Commerce Department put Megaspeed, a Singapore-based customer, and six affiliates on its Entity List on Monday, and that Huang and Nvidia's general counsel are scheduled to appear before House committees on 1 and 2 December. Fox News also reported that California's attorney general said an investigation into whether Nvidia misled investors continues; that is the office's position as reported, and Nvidia has not been found at fault.

What remains unknown is what the independent committee will find and when it will report, how Congress will respond, and whether the new committee changes how Nvidia screens customers. Nvidia's filing gives no timetable.

What it means: Board-governance, export-control compliance and geopolitical-risk work: clients that depend on Nvidia supply will ask for continuity plans, and companies selling advanced chips abroad will review their customer screening.

What we don't know yet

  • Outcome of the House appearances scheduled for 1 and 2 December
