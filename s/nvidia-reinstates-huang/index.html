Nvidia board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO 11 days after he resigned

Confirmed · 5 min

Open in the full site →

Nvidia's board on Monday 23 November 2026 unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and director, effective immediately, according to the company's filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. Huang resigned on 12 November, as covered earlier in this edition, so the reversal came after 11 days. Chief financial officer Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, returns to her CFO role, and Harvey C. Jones remains board chair. Euronews quotes Jones saying the board believes Huang is the right person to lead the company.

In the filing, Nvidia says the board received input from senior levels of the US government and concluded that Huang's return poses no national security risk. Engadget reports that the board met late on Sunday and voted on Monday, that Jones had said days earlier the board did not intend to reinstate Huang, and that President Donald Trump told CNBC he had influenced the decision. Euronews reports that Trump called the outcome the correct and proper decision on social media and said he had been speaking to the board directly and indirectly. Nvidia's own filing does not mention the president.

The board paired the decision with governance changes. A committee of independent directors, advised by independent counsel, is investigating reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, and Huang supports the work and will cooperate, the filing says. The board is also creating a National Security and Export Control Committee made up only of independent directors, and is hiring a chief compliance officer who will report to the nominating and corporate governance committee. The filing gives no timetable for the investigation.

The market reaction was large. Gadget Review coverage syndicated by Yahoo Finance reported Nvidia shares up about 30% to 33% in early trading, with the Nasdaq Composite up roughly 6% to 7%, the S&P 500 up roughly 5% and the Dow up about 3%. Euronews said the move put the stock on track to add more than $1 trillion of market value, possibly a first. These are intraday figures from press reports, not closing prices. The Associated Press, as quoted in the same coverage, said Nvidia had fallen nearly 23% in the week after the resignation and was trading near $167 after the surge, still below its $186 level before the controversy. The S&P 500 had lost seven sessions in a row before Monday, and Deutsche Bank strategist Jim Reid noted that comparable volatility shocks have historically taken several weeks to settle.

The decision sits inside a wider export-control story. The Commerce Department added Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer, and six affiliates to its Entity List on the same day, covered on the Policy page. The Next Web reports that four US departments had wanted the listing for months and that Senator Pete Ricketts pressed Commerce to explain the delay; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said publicly that Commerce holds the pen. Fox Business reports that Nvidia's own website had listed Megaspeed as a preferred cloud partner, and that Nvidia placed it on its internal no-sell list in June 2025.

The Entity List rule itself, per Tech Times, adds seven entities in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand, applies a presumption of denial to licence requests, and is the first new listing in 410 days. Shipments under earlier orders may proceed for 30 days, and the rule is scheduled for Federal Register publication on 25 November. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing, and a joint Commerce and Justice Department statement on 16 November announced no charges against the company, according to Tech Times. Euronews adds that Nvidia had reported better-than-expected quarterly results the week before the reinstatement.

Political scrutiny continues. Tech Times reports that Huang and Nvidia's general counsel are scheduled to appear before House committees on 1 and 2 December, and Euronews describes Huang's session as a closed, transcribed interview. Fox News also reported that California's attorney general said an investigation into whether Nvidia misled investors continues; that is the office's position as reported, and Nvidia has not been found at fault. Huang said he was honoured and would lead with humility, Euronews reports.

For clients, the lesson is about concentration. Nvidia's supply sits underneath most large AI build-outs, and a two-week leadership scare moved major indices. What remains unknown is what the independent committee will find and when it will report, how Congress will respond to the new listing and the hearings, and whether the new committee changes how Nvidia screens customers in practice.

What it means: Board-governance, export-control compliance and geopolitical-risk work: clients that depend on Nvidia supply will ask for continuity plans, and companies selling advanced chips abroad will review their customer screening.
