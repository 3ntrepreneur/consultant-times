McKinsey and Cognition ally to sell AI software engineering

Confirmed · 3 min

Open in the full site →

McKinsey & Company and Cognition, the maker of the Devin autonomous software engineer, announced an alliance on 16 November 2026, according to McKinsey's alliances blog. McKinsey says it will help clients prioritise high-value engineering work, set baselines for cost, speed and quality, and scale new ways of working, while Cognition supplies Devin, which it describes as model-agnostic.

McKinsey cites its own second-quarter 2026 survey of 334 product and engineering leaders: the top 25% reported productivity gains of more than 2x, while nearly 30% reported declining productivity. The firm also says it has helped more than 50 companies rewire for AI-powered software development. The Next Web, which reported the news the same day, says the two will sell the offering to large companies in the US and Europe. Cognition's claims of lower cost through model routing and of clients among large banks and insurers are the company's own and were not independently verified.

A McKinsey senior partner, Saptarshi Ganguly, is quoted saying doubling productivity takes more than deploying agents. The page does not state financial terms, an effective date beyond publication, or whether the alliance is exclusive.

The read-across for the industry is that a leading strategy firm is tying its change-management and baselining work to a specific agent vendor. That is a template other firms are likely to copy with other vendors, and a reason for engineering leaders to ask what independent measurement they get.

What it means: Direct read-across for consulting: McKinsey is packaging implementation of AI coding agents, with baselines for cost, speed and quality, as a service line; rivals and clients' engineering leaders must decide who owns that work.
