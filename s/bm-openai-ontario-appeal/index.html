Ontario appeal court lets Canadian publishers' copyright suit against OpenAI proceed

Reported · 2 min

Open in the full site →

The Ontario Court of Appeal dismissed OpenAI's appeal on 13 November 2026, allowing a copyright lawsuit by Canadian news organisations to proceed in Ontario, according to BNN Bloomberg and Tech Times. The plaintiffs include The Canadian Press, Torstar, The Globe and Mail, Postmedia and CBC/Radio-Canada, and they allege OpenAI used their content to train ChatGPT without permission or payment.

OpenAI had argued the case belonged in a U.S. court, BNN Bloomberg reports; the appeal court found no evidence that a Canadian judgment would conflict with U.S. law. Tech Times says the panel found a real and substantial connection to Ontario, and that the case will be judged under Canadian law, which has no text-and-data-mining exception like U.S. fair use. The ruling decides venue only, not infringement; Tech Times reports the plaintiffs seek statutory damages of up to $20,000 per work across 10.65 million works, and that OpenAI may seek leave to appeal to the Supreme Court of Canada. The claims are unproven.
