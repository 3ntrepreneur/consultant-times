Italy's privacy regulator fines Meta EUR 805 million over Facebook's Android photo-gallery feature

Reported · 2 min

Open in the full site →

Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, announced on 14 November 2026 a EUR 805 million ($887 million) fine on Meta, according to TechRepublic and CyberInsider. The regulator found Facebook did not clearly explain, or obtain specific consent for, a gallery-suggestion feature that was on by default and affected about 20 million Android users, the outlets report.

According to CyberInsider, the Garante found a violation of Article 122 of Italy's Privacy Code and ordered Meta to switch the feature off by default and seek separate consent within 60 days. Meta disputes the decision and plans to appeal, the outlets say; TechRepublic reports it argued that Ireland's regulator should handle the case, which Italy rejected, and CyberInsider quotes it calling the fine disproportionate. TechRepublic adds that the regulator denied that the material was used for AI training. The findings are the regulator's; Meta contests them.

What it means: Consent-flow and permission-design reviews, privacy-by-default audits of app features, and regulatory-strategy work on which EU authority has jurisdiction over big platforms.

What we don't know yet

  • Whether other EU regulators will examine the same feature
