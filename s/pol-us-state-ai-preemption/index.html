Washington's push to override state AI laws: what the order does and doesn't do

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

On 11 December 2025 President Trump signed an executive order titled Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence. It directs the Attorney General to create an AI Litigation Task Force whose job is to challenge state AI laws, on grounds such as interference with interstate commerce or conflict with federal law. The Commerce Department was told to review state AI laws and refer potentially unlawful provisions to the task force, with a list due in March 2026.

The order also asks White House advisers to draft a legislative proposal for a single federal framework that would override conflicting state laws, while carving out areas such as child safety, data-centre infrastructure and state government use of AI. It singled out Colorado's algorithmic-discrimination law as an early target.

Legal analysts stress the limits. An executive order is not a statute, so on its own it does not displace state law. Until courts invalidate a state rule, companies must keep complying with it. A February 2026 analysis notes that Colorado's AI Act (effective 30 June 2026) and California's frontier-AI transparency law (effective 1 January 2026) were unlikely to be affected in the short term.

The order has drawn criticism from labour groups and from politicians in both parties, and support from some policy analysts who want a single national standard. For clients that operate across states, the practical task is to build compliance that survives either result.

What it means: Multi-state clients need a compliance plan that works under both outcomes: state rules stay, or a federal standard replaces them. Scenario planning and regulatory monitoring are the work.

What we don't know yet

  • How the courts will rule on any federal challenge to a state AI law.
  • Whether Congress passes a federal framework, and what it would preempt.
  • The current status of Colorado's start date: we have not re-checked it since the February 2026 analysis cited here.
