Sila signs multi-year silicon anode offtake with Panasonic Energy worth over $1 billion

Reported · 2 min

Open in the full site →

Sila's release, dated 19 November, says the multi-year agreement covers its silicon-carbon anode material, made at the Moses Lake, Washington, plant, for use in Panasonic cells at US sites. It cites potential for over $1 billion of offtake over time but gives no volumes or start date; supply begins once the material is qualified.

Battery-Tech, which dates the announcement 18 November, adds that the companies plan to collaborate on intellectual property and that Sila expects about 500 manufacturing and construction jobs at Moses Lake from scaling for this and other commitments. It notes the deal follows a $1.4 billion loan commitment to Sila from the US Department of Defense's Office of Strategic Capital.

The headline value is described as potential rather than committed, so actual revenue depends on qualification and Panasonic's volumes.

What it means: Supply-chain localisation and qualification planning for battery materials; offtake-contract structuring and plant ramp-up support; capital-planning for US battery-materials build-outs.

What we don't know yet

  • No volumes, pricing or qualification timetable were disclosed
  • Sources differ by one day on the announcement date (18 vs 19 November)
