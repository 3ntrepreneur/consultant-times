Palo Alto Networks buys Applied Compute for $4.5B in stock

Confirmed · 4 min

Open in the full site →

Palo Alto Networks said on 18 November 2026 that it has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Applied Compute, an AI start-up. According to the company's press release filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, the all-stock transaction is valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments, and is expected to close in the company's fiscal third quarter of 2027, subject to customary conditions including regulatory clearance.

Applied Compute's co-founder and chief executive, Yash Patil, will become co-chief AI officer of Palo Alto Networks after the close. In the release, chairman and CEO Nikesh Arora said every enterprise will run a mix of open and closed models and that each needs securing. SiliconANGLE reports that Applied Compute raised $350 million at a $3.25 billion valuation earlier in November and has annual recurring revenue above $100 million; the revenue figure is the company's own claim.

The deal landed alongside Palo Alto's first-quarter results for the period ended 31 October. SiliconANGLE reports adjusted earnings of $1.05 a share against analyst expectations of $0.97, and revenue of $3.44 billion, up 39% from a year earlier. Next-generation security annual recurring revenue rose 66% to $9.73 billion. The company raised full-year revenue guidance to $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion from $14.10 billion to $14.20 billion given in September, and guided full-year adjusted earnings per share to $4.26 to $4.30. The earnings figures come from SiliconANGLE and were not checked against the full results release.

Both SiliconANGLE and a separate market wrap report that Palo Alto's shares rose sharply on the day; the wrap put the gain at about 10% in regular trading. The release does not give a premium paid over Applied Compute's last round, but the $4.5 billion price compares with the $3.25 billion valuation reported for the recent funding.

The pattern matters for advisers. Acquirers are paying for post-training and inference capability, not only models, and are positioning security as part of the AI stack.
