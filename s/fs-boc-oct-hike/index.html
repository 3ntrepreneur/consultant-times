Bank of Canada hikes to 2.5%, its first increase in more than three years

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

The Bank of Canada raised its overnight target by 25 basis points to 2.5% on 28 October, the first increase since July 2023. The Bank Rate moves to 2.75% and the deposit rate to 2.45%. The Canadian Press reports that Governor Tiff Macklem said the Bank considered holding, because the rise in inflation is narrowly concentrated around an energy price shock, but moved to limit the risk of cost pressure spreading to other prices.

The October Monetary Policy Report projects GDP growth of 1% in 2026 and 2% in both 2027 and 2028, a stronger path than in July. EconoTimes reports it sees inflation averaging about 3% in 2027 and returning near 2% by mid-2028. Statistics Canada put September inflation at 3.1%, while the Bank's gasoline-excluded measure was 2.2%.

Forecasters differ on what comes next. CIBC expects a pause on 9 December and a further quarter-point move in January. RBC's chief economist expects another hike in December or January.

What it means: Lenders, insurers and mortgage-exposed clients now plan for a rising-rate path. Net interest margin, credit-loss and housing-demand assumptions all need refreshing.

What we don't know yet

  • The path of oil prices, which the Bank ties to its inflation risk, is uncertain.
  • Forecaster views on December and January are opinions, not Bank guidance.
