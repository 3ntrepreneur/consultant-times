Canada's Budget 2026 sets aside funding to modernise federal IT and adopt AI

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

According to BNN Bloomberg and CTV News, Budget 2026 sets aside funding over five years for a Government Transformation Fund meant to speed up federal IT projects and the adoption of artificial intelligence inside departments.

A new body, Digital Transformation Canada, would lead AI and emerging-technology adoption across the public service. Reporting describes it as combining Shared Services Canada and the Canadian Digital Service, with a Digital Transformation Canada Act to be introduced. A 7.6 million, five-year fellowship programme would bring private-sector and academic experts into government for short stints.

The same package carries other digital lines, including 05 million over six years for modernising benefits delivery at Employment and Social Development Canada, and 92.5 million over four years for the Canada Border Services Agency, plus 2.5 million a year after that, as reported.

The money sits alongside restraint. The government says it is on track to shrink the federal public service by 10% by 2029; the reporting puts headcount at 332,000 in August 2026, down from a 2023-24 peak of 368,000, with operating cuts of 00 million over four years and 00 million a year after.

Both outlets appear to carry the same underlying article, so the figures are effectively single-sourced and should be checked against the budget documents. What is not yet known is how the fund will be allocated, how much will go to outside suppliers, and when the proposed legislation will be tabled.

What it means: Public-sector IT, AI-governance and operating-model work is funded, but the same budget squeezes departmental operating spending, so advisers should expect scrutiny of external spend.
