Clariant and CD&R confirm talks to buy Ashland

Reported · 3 min

Open in the full site →

Clariant confirmed on Monday 23 November 2026 that it is in discussions about a possible acquisition of Ashland in partnership with private equity firm Clayton, Dubilier & Rice (CD&R). In an ad hoc announcement from Muttenz, Switzerland, the company said it was responding to market speculation. It disclosed no price or terms and said there is no certainty that a transaction will be agreed or completed, adding that it will make a further announcement if and when appropriate.

Reuters reported that Bloomberg News had said on Sunday that the two were negotiating an all-cash deal, and gave a value of about $3.39 billion, close to Ashland's recent trading price. Ashland's market capitalisation is $3.32 billion according to LSEG data cited by Reuters, and its shares rose about 1% in extended trading. The market value has moved around: Personal Care Insights cited about $3.5 billion in August, and Mergermarket's Activist Monitor put it near $3.14 billion in October at a share price of about $68.76.

The background is an activist-driven sale process. Personal Care Insights reported in August, citing Bloomberg, that Ashland was exploring a sale after months of activist pressure and was talking to strategic and financial buyers, with Citigroup and Lazard advising. Ancora Holdings, which holds about 4.24% according to Mergermarket, went public in June pushing for a sale, and Cruiser Capital Advisors, with a smaller stake, joined it. Both reportedly threatened proxy fights if there was no progress by the September director-nomination deadline. At the end of July Ashland reached a cooperation agreement with Ancora that added two directors and created a capital-allocation advisory committee.

Mergermarket reported on 5 October, citing three sources, that initial bids were collected on 2 October, and, citing two sources, that CD&R had teamed with Clariant. Those sources said Clariant alone would face antitrust issues because the businesses overlap in personal care, which would explain the private equity partner. Apollo and Carlyle were also named as interested, with Advent and Standard Industries, a roughly 9.9% holder, mentioned in earlier Bloomberg coverage. None of the parties commented in those reports, and these are source-based accounts, not confirmed facts. Mergermarket also noted that Ashland is described as a reluctant seller and that sponsors might struggle to pay a premium of more than 10% to 15%.

On valuation, Mergermarket reports that Ancora's June presentation set a base case of 11.5 times estimated fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA, or about $76.47 a share, and a bull case of 13.8 times, or about $96. The shares closed near $68.76 in early October. Ashland reported fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $385 million, and its release credits its Life Sciences segment with a record quarterly margin and says Specialty Additives was held back by plant productivity. Whether a deal near the reported $3.39 billion would satisfy those holders is not known.

Ashland's own numbers show what a buyer would be underwriting. For the fiscal year ended 30 September 2026, reported on 3 November, sales were $1.863 billion, up 2%, and adjusted EBITDA was $385 million, down from $401 million, a margin of 20.7%. Fourth-quarter sales were $498 million and adjusted EBITDA $121 million. Life Sciences posted a 35.6% segment margin in the quarter, Personal Care sales rose 8%, and Specialty Additives EBITDA fell 31% on lower productivity at its Hopewell plant. Year-end net debt was $891 million, a leverage ratio of 2.3 times. For fiscal 2027 the company guided to sales of $1.905 billion to $1.975 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $405 million to $435 million. The release does not mention a sale process.

Clariant is itself under margin pressure. Its third-quarter local-currency sales rose 5.6% to CHF 968.8 million, but EBITDA margin before exceptionals fell to 15.6% from 17.9% as raw materials rose 12%, energy 9% and logistics 23.7%, according to Indian Chemical News. It reported CHF 79 million of run-rate savings against a CHF 90 million target for 2026, and kept its guidance for a margin of about 18%. For scale, Clariant's release states 10,281 employees and CHF 3.915 billion of sales in 2025.

The unknowns are the structure of the Clariant and CD&R partnership, which assets each would own, the price, whether rival bidders remain, and how competition authorities would view the personal-care overlap.

What it means: Due diligence, synergy sizing and portfolio-review work for the bidders, separation and carve-out planning if assets are sold on, and defence and strategic-alternatives work for Ashland's board.

What we don't know yet

  • Price, structure and the split of assets between Clariant and CD&R
  • Whether other private equity bidders remain in the process
  • Antitrust view of the personal-care overlap and timing
