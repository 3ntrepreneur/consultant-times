Software buyers expect AI budgets to rise, but vendors struggle to price it

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

Simon-Kucher's Global Software Study 2026, reported by Consultancy.eu on 25 November 2026, surveyed about 500 software executives and 200 buyers. Some 79% of executives say AI is already boosting revenue, but 62% see at least moderate risk that AI replaces their core products within two years. Among buyers, 7% have already replaced a traditional tool with an AI alternative and 36% have built or piloted substitutes.

Buyers say customer service (53%), workplace productivity (43%) and data analysis (42%) are most exposed. On budgets, 86% of buyers expect AI software spend to rise at least 10% over two years, against 43% for traditional software. Yet only 4% measure AI impact across the whole company, and two-thirds require measured financial return before approving purchases.

On the vendor side, 94% have changed or plan to change pricing, and executives expect a median 20% rise in delivery costs over two years, while only 23% track AI delivery cost per account or task. Only 23% charge separately for AI-agent access; 43% of buyers prefer agent access bundled into plan tiers. Figures are from a consultancy's own survey and no primary report link was given.

What it means: AI pricing and packaging redesign, unit-economics tracking for AI delivery cost, and buyer-side business-case and governance work.
