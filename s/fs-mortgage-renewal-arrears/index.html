Mortgage arrears are rising slowly, concentrated in Toronto and Vancouver, while wider household credit holds up

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

CMHC's Housing Observer says the national mortgage arrears rate, measured as 90 or more days behind, rose 7 basis points between the third quarter of 2023 and the third quarter of 2025 and remains low by historical standards. Its model, based on Equifax data, expects moderate increases from late 2025 to late 2026, with Toronto and Vancouver the most at risk.

Canadian Mortgage Professional reports CIBC economists wrote that household credit quality is surprising on the upside, with credit scores improving and early-stage delinquencies easing even as serious mortgage arrears climb. Consumer proposals made up 77% of insolvency filings in the third quarter against 60% in 2019. CIBC expects renewal payment increases to ease notably from February 2027.

What it means: Lenders can plan for a slow grind, not a cliff. Underwriting, collections and renewal-retention strategies matter most in the two most exposed metro markets.
