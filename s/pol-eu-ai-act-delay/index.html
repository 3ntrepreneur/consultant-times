EU pushes its toughest AI Act rules back to December 2027

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

The European Union has agreed to postpone the most demanding parts of its AI Act. Under the Digital Omnibus on AI, obligations for high-risk systems that stand alone now apply from 2 December 2027 instead of 2 August 2026. Rules for high-risk AI built into regulated products, such as machinery, move to 2 August 2028, according to law-firm summaries of the agreement.

High-risk systems are the ones used in areas such as hiring, education, credit and insurance, law enforcement, biometrics and critical infrastructure. The delay was justified by missing technical standards and guidance: companies argued they could not comply with rules that did not yet have a rulebook behind them. The European Commission first proposed the change in November 2025, and Parliament and the Council reached a deal in 2026.

The deal is narrower than the headline suggests. Practitioner analysis notes that the bans on certain AI practices (in force since February 2025) and the rules for general-purpose models (since August 2025) were not touched, and that transparency duties in Article 50 still apply from August 2026. The Omnibus mostly streamlines and clarifies the existing text rather than rewriting its risk-based structure.

For clients, the practical point is timing. Teams that use the extra months to build inventories of their AI systems, classify them by risk and set up governance will be ahead of the queue for conformity assessments in 2027. Those that treat the delay as a cancellation will face a compressed deadline later.

What it means: Banks, insurers, HR-tech and health clients get more time, but still need AI inventories, risk classification and governance programmes. Expect work to be re-phased, not cancelled.

What we don't know yet

  • Whether every detail of the agreed text has completed formal adoption.
  • How quickly the harmonised technical standards will actually be published.
