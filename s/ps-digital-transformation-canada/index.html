Ottawa launches Digital Transformation Canada and budgets $1 billion for government IT

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

Digital Transformation Canada was launched on 3 September, led by CEO Patrick Pichette, a former Bell Canada and McKinsey executive. Its own site says it brings together Shared Services Canada and selected functions from the Treasury Board Secretariat, Public Services and Procurement Canada, and Employment and Social Development Canada.

Budget 2026, tabled 5 November, proposes about $1 billion over five years from 2026-27 for a Government Transformation Fund, $17.6 million over five years for a private-sector fellowship, and a future Digital Transformation Canada Act. Gowling WLG also notes $176.3 million over five years for a Digital Safety and Data Protection Commission. CTV reports the GCtranslate tool has translated more than 400 million words.

What it means: A large, long-running public-sector tech-modernisation market. Procurement, change-management and AI-adoption work sit inside it.

What we don't know yet

  • Budget measures are proposals until Parliament passes them.
  • Operating budget for the new body is not published.
