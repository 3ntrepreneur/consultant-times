KFC franchisee Tasty Chick'n files Chapter 11 with 84 restaurants

Reported · 3 min

Open in the full site →

Tasty Chick'n LLC and three affiliates filed for Chapter 11 protection on 16 November 2026 in the US Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of Texas, according to National Restaurant News and TheStreet (via Yahoo). The operator runs 84 restaurants in ten states: 75 KFC units and nine KFC/Taco Bell co-branded units.

The filings put numbers on the squeeze. NRN reports nearly $73 million of funded debt. TheStreet, citing court papers, reports assets of $50 million to $100 million and liabilities of $100 million to $500 million. The company's papers say sales fell 11.4% in fiscal 2024, 1.4% in 2025 and 2.8% so far in 2026, while restaurant-level margin dropped from 11.8% in fiscal 2023 to 4% this year. Trailing restaurant EBITDA was about $7.1 million against $12.4 million a year earlier, per TheStreet.

The papers also describe a dispute with the franchisor. Royalty deferrals began in mid-2025, a notice of breach followed in December 2025, and a notice of intent to terminate was issued on 15 September 2026, with unpaid royalties and fees cited at roughly $15.6 million to $15.7 million. The company also points to franchisor-required remodels at nearly 40 restaurants through 2029 as a cost it says it cannot carry.

To fund the case, the company has arranged a debtor-in-possession facility of up to $20 million from its prepetition lender, MidCap Financial Trust: $5 million of new money and a $15 million roll-up. No reorganisation plan has been described; the stated aim is a court-supervised sale, either as one portfolio or market by market. These are the company's claims in court filings, and the franchisor's position has not been reported in the sources we read.

For advisers, the case is a template: a mid-size franchisee squeezed between falling traffic, rising costs and brand-mandated capital spending. It points to work for lenders and landlords valuing a sale by market, for franchisors deciding how hard to enforce remodel schedules, and for other multi-unit operators stress-testing their own covenants.

What it means: Turnaround, franchise-system and real-estate advisory: franchisee distress, remodel mandates and royalty disputes are a pattern franchisors and lenders must plan for.
