Alibaba is sued by shareholders over alleged under-disclosure of export-control risk tied to Megaspeed

Reported · 2 min

Open in the full site →

A shareholder filed a proposed class action on 19 November 2026 in federal court in Manhattan against Alibaba, alleging it inflated its share price by understating, in its 2024, 2025 and 2026 annual reports, the risk that actual or suspected export-control violations could harm its business, according to the Manila Times. The suit covers buyers of Alibaba's ADSs from 23 May 2024 to 11 November 2026 and seeks unspecified damages. FourWeekMBA reports that CEO Eddie Wu is also named.

The complaint points to Alibaba's reported ties to Megaspeed, a Singapore company suspected of helping Beijing access advanced Nvidia chips, including reports that Alibaba became its dominant customer, the outlet says. Alibaba shares fell 14% over four days, partly after a 12 November Reuters report on possible tighter U.S. limits on remote chip use. FourWeekMBA says lead-plaintiff motions are due by 19 January 2027. These are allegations; no court has ruled, and Alibaba had not responded when the outlets published.

What it means: Export-control and sanctions compliance reviews, third-party and customer due-diligence programmes, and risk-disclosure advice for firms buying or reselling advanced AI compute.

What we don't know yet
