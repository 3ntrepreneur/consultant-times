EY Canada puts $80 million into AI, quantum and future-of-work bets

Reported · 3 min

Open in the full site →

EY Canada has invested $80 million in AI innovation, future-of-work programmes and what it calls Canadian moonshot ideas, according to Consulting.ca on 20 November 2026, which cites the firm's 2026 Impact Report. The spend includes an on-site quantum computer at its applied innovation centre.

The report also gives people figures: employees logged 450,000 learning hours last year, about 50 each, and more than 4,100 volunteers gave time to projects that reached 3.1 million people, including an 'AI for Good' programme. EY Canada became a sustaining supporter of the First Nations Major Projects Coalition over the year. Consulting.ca puts the firm at more than 8,300 people across audit, tax and consulting.

Alycia Calvert, EY Canada's chair and chief executive, is quoted saying Canada has no shortage of ambition. Chief technology officer Biren Agnihotri describes pairing experimentation with skills and confidence. The coverage does not say over what period the $80 million was spent, how it splits between AI, quantum and other programmes, or what revenue it is meant to support.

For the market, the notable point is the on-site quantum hardware: few Canadian advisers publicise it. Whether clients pay for quantum-readiness work, rather than treating it as a showcase, is not yet visible in the reporting.

What it means: Signals where a Big Four firm is spending to sell AI and quantum-readiness work to Canadian clients; buyers can expect pilots and joint-innovation offers, and rivals face pressure to match the investment story.

What we don't know yet

  • Time period and split of the $80 million
  • Revenue or client-work targets tied to the spend
  • The Impact Report was not linked, so figures are as relayed by Consulting.ca
