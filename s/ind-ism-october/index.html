US factory index rises to 55.5 in October, the tenth straight month of growth

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

The Institute for Supply Management's manufacturing PMI rose to 55.5 in October from 54.5, beating forecasts and marking a tenth month of expansion. Production hit 59.0, new orders 55.5 and employment 52.4. The prices-paid index eased to 75.2. Reuters, via Colorado Biz, ties demand to consumer spending and AI-related business spending.

S&P Global's separate survey showed US manufacturing growing more slowly: its final October PMI was 54.9, down from 55.9 in September, a 52-month high. Respondent comments in ISM's report describe demand strength in AI, data centres, semiconductors and defence, but also customers in wait-and-see mode and cost, lead-time and policy uncertainty.

What it means: Manufacturers and their suppliers see healthy demand, led by AI, data centres and defence, alongside cost and policy pressure. Operations and procurement work stay in demand.

What we don't know yet

  • Survey data, not output data. The two surveys differ in size and method.
