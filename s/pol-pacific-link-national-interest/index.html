Pacific Link becomes the first project listed under the Building Canada Act

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

The federal government listed Pacific Link as a project of national interest on 1 October, its first under the Building Canada Act. The roughly 1,250-kilometre line would carry up to a million barrels a day from Bruderheim, Alberta, to near Delta, B.C. Alberta puts the cost at C$35.2 billion to C$43.7 billion. The Major Projects Office leads the review, with a conditions document due by 1 September 2027.

CBC, via Wealth Professional, reports Alberta and Trans Mountain would each hold 45% and Pembina 10%, with Indigenous communities offered at least 10% ownership backed by loan guarantees. Baird Maritime reports the Coldwater Indian Band is considering court action. Carney said the project's emissions intensity would be addressed partly through the Pathways carbon capture project.

What it means: Energy, infrastructure and Indigenous-partnership advisers get a test of Ottawa's one-window review model. Financing structure and consultation are the risks.

What we don't know yet

  • Final route and regulatory approvals.
  • Whether Indigenous consultation and B.C. support hold.
