Three in four Canadian AI leaders fear falling behind, IBM survey finds

Reported · 3 min

Open in the full site →

Three-quarters of senior AI leaders in Canada say their organisations risk falling behind competitors at their current pace, according to Consulting.ca on 19 November 2026, reporting research by IBM and Oxford Economics. The survey covered 1,667 chief AI officers and equivalent leaders across 33 geographies and was fielded from April to June 2026.

The Canadian findings point to organisational friction more than technology. Only 15% of Canadian respondents say AI leaders hold full authority over AI investment decisions, against 18% globally. Seventy per cent say their organisations do not quickly shift decisions and resources when performance measurement shows an opportunity, and 73% say governance, risk and compliance considerations delay action.

Scaling is the recurring sticking point: 72% say transformation stalls when successful pilots are scaled, 66% cite embedding AI in core workflows, and 64% cite turning strategy into execution. At the same time, 42% report that AI agents already start and carry out actions on their own across workflows. Matthew Wetmore of IBM Canada said successful pilots alone will not deliver transformation.

What it means: AI governance and risk operating models, pilot-to-production scaling programmes and decision-rights redesign for Canadian enterprises; a ready client opener for IBM Canada, Big Four and boutique AI practices.
