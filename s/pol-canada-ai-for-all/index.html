Canada's AI plan: big spending, no AI law, new privacy and online-safety bills

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

Prime Minister Mark Carney and AI Minister Evan Solomon launched Canada's national AI strategy, AI for All, on 4 June 2026. It proposes large-scale data centres, a sovereign AI foundation and measures to attract foreign investment into Canadian AI, and a Canada Trusted AI Certification programme to identify trustworthy products in the market.

The notable choice is what it leaves out. Law-firm and commentary analysis agrees that the government does not plan AI-specific regulation. Instead it relies on updating privacy law and bringing back online-safety legislation, a different path from the EU's dedicated AI Act. An earlier federal bill with AI rules, the Artificial Intelligence and Data Act, died with the previous Parliament, and commentators note the government does not appear to intend to reintroduce AI-specific legislation.

Parliament now has a set of related bills. Solomon tabled a long-awaited private-sector privacy bill with stiff fines in June, and the government has also tabled the Safe Social Media Act, which would create a Digital Safety Act. Solomon has said he is confident the bills together will protect children, personal information and privacy.

Critics, including law professor Michael Geist, have called the strategy big on spending but short on the specifics that matter, such as transparency requirements. For Canadian companies the message is to prepare for stronger privacy duties, and to expect certification and procurement rules to shape which AI products public bodies buy.

What it means: Canadian clients face privacy and data-governance obligations first, not an AI-specific regime. Demand: privacy programmes, AI governance and assurance, and sovereign-compute strategy.

What we don't know yet

  • When the privacy and online-safety bills will pass, and in what form.
  • How the Trusted AI Certification will work and who will be required to hold it.
