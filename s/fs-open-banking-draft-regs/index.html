Canada's open-banking rules move to draft regulations, but no launch date is set

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

On 27 June the federal government published proposed Consumer-Driven Banking Regulations in the Canada Gazette, Part I. Substance Law's summary says the draft covers accreditation, complaint handling, technical-standards compliance, consent management and national-security review, with public comment open until 26 August.

Open Banking Expo reports that, in March, the Bank of Canada's payments lead said the Bank was still scoping the work and wanted industry to have enough lead time before any date is set. Phase one is read access to account data; payments come later.

What it means: Banks, fintechs and payments firms need accreditation, consent and data-sharing programmes. Timing risk is the planning problem.

What we don't know yet

  • No confirmed launch date was found in the sources used.
  • Final regulations may differ from the draft.
