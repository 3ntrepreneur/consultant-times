Treasury fines Bank of America more than $1.2 billion over money-laundering and sanctions failures

Confirmed · 2 min

Open in the full site →

The U.S. Treasury announced on 13 October 2026 penalties of more than $1.21 billion against Bank of America: $1.2 billion from FinCEN, a $750 million civil penalty from the OCC and an $11.6 million OFAC settlement. According to Treasury, the bank admitted to willfully violating the Bank Secrecy Act and failed to timely report more than $5 billion in suspicious transactions, with the OCC saying failures continued after a December 2024 cease-and-desist order.

Treasury says the FinCEN consent order requires a third-party lookback and independent reviews of monitoring, customer due diligence, foreign correspondent due diligence and data governance. OFAC cited 475 apparent sanctions violations. Al Jazeera reports that the OCC fine is the largest in its history and that Bank of America said it is responsible for getting financial crime compliance right and is working to address the issues. The conduct described is as alleged by the regulators and partly admitted in the FinCEN order.

What it means: AML lookbacks and independent reviews, transaction-monitoring and sanctions-screening remediation, and data-governance programmes; read-across work for peer banks on correspondent-banking controls.

What we don't know yet

  • Whether other regulators or private claims will follow was not reported
