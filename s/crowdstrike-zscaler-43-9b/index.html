CrowdStrike to buy Zscaler in $43.9 billion all-stock deal

Confirmed · 5 min

Open in the full site →

CrowdStrike said on Monday 23 November 2026 that it will acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction that values the network-security company at about $43.9 billion, based on CrowdStrike's closing price on 20 November. According to the joint release, each Zscaler share converts into 1.135 CrowdStrike shares, worth about $260.41 on that basis and a premium of roughly 34% to Zscaler's 20 November close. Both boards approved the deal unanimously. Zscaler founder Jay Chaudhry and affiliates, who hold about 34% of Zscaler's shares, signed voting agreements.

Because the price is paid in shares, Zscaler holders will own about 15.9% of the combined company and CrowdStrike holders about 84.1%. Closing is expected in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, which falls in calendar 2027, and needs a Zscaler shareholder vote, regulatory approvals and customary conditions. CrowdStrike will file a Form S-4 containing the proxy statement and prospectus. TechCrunch reported a close around this time next year, which differs slightly from the company's own timing, so this article uses the release. The two companies run separately until then, and Chaudhry will continue to lead the Zscaler business and join the CrowdStrike board.

The companies were also reporting results the same day: CrowdStrike its fiscal third quarter and Zscaler its fiscal first quarter. They moved a joint call to 5:00 a.m. Pacific on 23 November, replacing one that had been set for 1 December. The releases point readers to the companies' investor sites for the quarterly figures, and this article does not report them.

The commercial argument is about reach and control. CrowdStrike says the combined business will have more than $10 billion of annual recurring revenue at close, of which Zscaler reported about $3.9 billion in its latest quarter, and that the deal expands its addressable market by more than $185 billion in calendar 2029. The two share more than 2,000 customers and 16 integrations, while about 8,000 Zscaler customers do not use CrowdStrike, according to the release. Zscaler says its platform protects half of the Fortune 500, operates from more than 200 locations and processes more than 750 billion transactions a day; those are company figures. CrowdStrike plans to carry Zscaler products on its single-contract Falcon Flex licence and to feed network telemetry into its Falcon Next-Gen SIEM. George Kurtz, CrowdStrike's founder and chief executive, framed the deal around securing AI and a single control plane, and Chaudhry described it as the natural evolution of the two companies' partnership, in the release.

The synergy targets are the part to watch. CrowdStrike expects about $1 billion of annualised revenue run-rate within 30 months of closing, and $500 million of annualised cost run-rate within 18 to 24 months, mainly from data-centre infrastructure, vendors and wider operations. Those are the buyer's targets, not results. The release says the deal should be significantly and immediately accretive to free cash flow per share before synergies, and accretive to revenue growth, non-GAAP operating margin and free cash flow margin within the first fiscal year after close once synergies are counted.

Outside analysts framed it competitively. Cybersecurity Dive quotes Madelein van der Hout of Forrester saying the deal fills a network gap in CrowdStrike's offer, and Shaul Eyal of TD Cowen saying it helps the company against Palo Alto Networks. In a longer Forrester note published on 24 November, principal analyst James Plouffe calls it the largest cybersecurity acquisition to date, puts Zscaler at about 11,000 customers with about 8,000 not overlapping with CrowdStrike, and notes that CrowdStrike's last three acquisitions, SGNL, Seraphic and Dreadnode, totalled about $1.2 billion, which makes this one uncharacteristically large. Cybersecurity Dive adds that CrowdStrike also agreed this month to buy Fiddler, and that the Wall Street Journal reported prices of $95 million for Dreadnode and $400 million for Fiddler; the companies did not release terms.

Forrester sees shareholder approval as a formality given Chaudhry's 34%, and regulatory risk as higher in the European Union and United Kingdom, where investment screening could add filings. It says regulators might look at bundling through Falcon Flex and at endpoint interoperability with Microsoft Defender and SentinelOne, and judges that interoperability commitments are a likelier outcome than a block. It flags integration risk too: CrowdStrike has not integrated a company this size, Zscaler's cloud raises questions about data-centre consolidation and outage risk given CrowdStrike's July 2024 outage, and ownership of Red Canary raises vendor-neutrality questions for managed-detection customers who use other endpoint tools. Forrester lists product overlaps in cloud and SaaS posture management, data security, identity and browser security, and notes that more than 20 vendors claim secure access service edge capabilities, so Netskope, Cato Networks and others may see partnerships shift. That is one analyst firm's view, not a forecast of what regulators will do.

For buyers, Forrester's advice is practical: ask for demonstrated interoperability, and use renewal talks to secure price protection and change-of-control terms. Qatalyst Partners is lead financial adviser to CrowdStrike, with J.P. Morgan and BofA Securities also advising and Davis Polk & Wardwell as legal counsel. BDT & MSD Partners is lead financial adviser to Zscaler, with Citigroup and Goldman Sachs also named and Wilson Sonsini Goodrich & Rosati as legal counsel.

What is not yet known is whether regulators will review the combination closely, how customers will respond to bundled pricing, how much of the $500 million cost target will fall on staff, and how the two architectures will be joined. The release does not state a termination fee.

What it means: Expect integration planning, cost take-out, vendor-consolidation and security-architecture work on both sides, plus CISOs re-running their vendor strategy and contract renewals as the combined platform pushes bundled pricing.

What we don't know yet

  • Termination fee and detailed deal protections are not stated in the release
  • Scope and timing of regulatory review
  • How many roles the $500 million cost target affects
  • How the Zscaler network architecture will be joined to the Falcon platform
