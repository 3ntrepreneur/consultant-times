UK banks face Basel 3.1 capital rules from 1 January 2027

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

The Prudential Regulation Authority published its final Basel 3.1 rules in January 2026, and they take effect in the UK on 1 January 2027, according to an explainer by consultancy Gini on Consultancy.uk on 25 November 2026. The start date moved several times, from January 2025 to July 2025, then January 2026, before settling on 2027.

The output floor stops a bank's modelled risk-weighted assets falling below a share of the standardised-approach figure. It starts at 60% on 1 January 2027 and rises to 65% in 2028, 70% in 2029 and 72.5% in 2030, according to Gini. Advanced internal-ratings models are removed for large corporates and financial institutions, income-producing real estate loses both internal-model options, and equity exposures move to the standardised approach, with existing permissions running to end-2029. Residential mortgages are split by loan-to-value, with the part up to 55% taking a 20% risk weight.

Operational risk moves to one standardised method and internal models for credit valuation adjustment are withdrawn, according to Gini. Market-risk standardised approaches start in January 2027 and the internal-models approach in January 2028. The PRA is also moving remaining capital rules into its Rulebook, including a Strong and Simple regime for small domestic deposit takers, broadly those under £20 billion of assets.

The explainer is by a consultancy that sells in this area and its footnotes were not available when read, so figures should be checked against the PRA's PS1/26 before client use.

What it means: Capital-impact modelling, risk-weighted-asset optimisation, data and reporting build, and programme management for UK banks and building societies; implementation assurance for smaller lenders.
