Target's Q3 sales rise 7.1%, helped by $301M tariff refund

Confirmed · 3 min

Open in the full site →

Target reported third-quarter results on 18 November 2026 for the quarter ended 31 October. According to the company's earnings release, filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, net sales were $27,067 million, up 7.1% from $25,270 million a year earlier. Comparable sales rose 5.6%, with stores up 4.6% and digital up 9.8%, and comparable traffic rose 5.0%.

The profit lines are flattered by a one-off. Target recognised a $301 million pretax benefit from refunds of tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, worth $0.50 a share. Reported diluted earnings per share were $2.65 against $1.51 a year earlier. Excluding the refund, the company says GAAP EPS would have risen 42% and adjusted EPS 21%. Operating income was $1,672 million, a 6.2% margin, against $948 million, or 3.8%, a year ago, and gross margin was 30.5% against 28.2%.

On the outlook, Target now expects full-year 2026 net sales growth in a range around 6%, one point higher than its prior range, and full-year EPS of $10.65 to $11.40, which includes about $2.14 a share of tariff-refund benefits recognised so far. Guidance excludes any future refunds. The company also set 2027 capital spending at $5 billion to $6 billion. Chief executive Michael Fiddelke said results reflect progress against strategic priorities. Month-to-date November sales are in line with guidance and accelerating from October, the company says.

The release does not state the prior guidance for operating margin or EPS, so the size of the change in those lines is unknown from this document. A market wrap on Yahoo Finance reported Target shares up about 4% on the day.

The lesson for clients is earnings quality. A large share of this quarter's profit growth is a tariff refund that will not repeat, and importers across retail may be owed similar money, which creates recovery and compliance work alongside the usual margin and capex planning.

What it means: Tariff-refund recovery and customs-compliance work for importers; margin-bridge and earnings-quality analysis for retail finance teams; capex planning as Target guides to $5–6B in 2027.
