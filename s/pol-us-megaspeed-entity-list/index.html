US adds Megaspeed and six affiliates to the Entity List over AI chip diversion risk

Reported · 3 min read

Open in the full site →

The US Commerce Department added Megaspeed and six other entities to its Entity List in a rule posted for public inspection on 23 November 2026, according to Tech Times. The entities are in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand. Exports to them now need a licence, with a presumption of denial, because of what the rule describes as a significant risk of diversion of advanced computing items. Tech Times reports it is the first new listing in 410 days.

Engadget reports that the listing bars US companies, Nvidia included, from selling items such as AI chips to Megaspeed or its affiliates without a licence. Commerce began investigating the company in 2024, and Nvidia placed it on its own no-sell list in June 2025, Engadget says. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing. Tech Times reports that a joint Commerce and Justice Department statement on 16 November announced no charges against the company.

Two dates matter for compliance teams. Shipments under earlier orders may proceed if completed within 30 days of public inspection, which runs to 23 December, and the rule is scheduled for Federal Register publication on 25 November. Tech Times also notes that the broader Affiliates Rule, which extends controls to unlisted subsidiaries, remains suspended until 10 January 2027, so unnamed affiliates are not automatically covered.

Congressional attention is continuing. Tech Times reports that Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang and the company's general counsel are scheduled to appear before House committees on 1 and 2 December. The sources reviewed do not say what legislation may follow or whether further entities will be listed.

What it means: Export-control compliance reviews, customer screening and know-your-customer work for chip makers, cloud providers and distributors, and supply-chain mapping for data-centre buyers in Southeast Asia.
